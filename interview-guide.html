For your first job, internship, or volunteer position

The 15 most common interview questions

  1. Tell me about yourself.Give a 30-second answer: your grade and school, 1 or 2 strengths, and why you want this job. Skip private details.
  2. Why do you want to work here?Name something you like about the business (friendly team, you shop there, you like kids) and how you can help.
  3. What are your strengths?Pick 2 strengths and prove each one with a quick example.
  4. What is one weakness?Choose a real but small weakness, then say what you are doing to improve it.
  5. Why should we hire you?Match your skills to what the job needs: “You need someone dependable, and I…”
  6. Tell me about a time you worked on a team.Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Say what you did.
  7. Tell me about a time you solved a problem.Use STAR. School, sports, family, and volunteering stories all count.
  8. How would you handle an upset customer?Stay calm, listen, say sorry, try to fix it, and get a manager if needed.
  9. What is your availability?Know your real days and hours before you go. Remember school-year hour limits for teens.
  10. How do you handle stress or a busy day?Show a plan: a planner, a to-do list, staying calm, and asking for help.
  11. Tell me about a mistake you made.Own it, explain how you fixed it, and share what you learned. Stay positive.
  12. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?Mention your goals (college, a trade, a career) and the skills this job will help you build.
  13. What do you know about our business?Look at their website or visit before the interview. Mention one thing you learned.
  14. How would a teacher or coach describe you?Use 2 or 3 words (reliable, kind, hard-working) and a quick reason.
  15. Do you have any questions for us?Always say yes! Ask 1 or 2 questions from the list below.

🙋 Questions to ask the interviewer

  • What does a typical day look like in this job?
  • What do your best employees do well?
  • What training will I get when I start?
  • What are the usual hours or shifts for this position?
  • What do you like most about working here?
  • What are the next steps, and when should I expect to hear back?

Wait to ask about pay until they offer the job or bring it up.

⭐ The STAR answer

  • Situation: where and when
  • Task: your job or the problem
  • Action: what you did, step by step
  • Result: what happened and what you learned

Practice out loud. A good answer takes about 1 to 2 minutes.

👕 What to wear

  • Dress one step nicer than the job’s uniform.
  • A clean collared shirt, polo, blouse, or simple sweater.
  • Clean pants, khakis, or a skirt with no rips.
  • Clean, closed-toe shoes.
  • Neat hair, light or no perfume, no hats.
  • You do not need to buy anything new. Clean and neat matters most.

🎒 What to bring

  • 2 or 3 copies of your resume in a folder
  • A pen and a small notebook
  • Your list of references (names and phone numbers)
  • Your weekly availability, written down
  • Your questions for the interviewer
  • A photo ID, if you have one

✅ Interview Day, Follow-Up, and Finding Jobs

Interview & Job Search Guide, page 2

📋 Day-of checklist

  • Know the address, the time, and the interviewer’s name
  • Plan your ride and arrive 10 minutes early
  • Phone on silent and put away
  • Go alone (a ride can wait outside)
  • Greet everyone politely, including the front desk
  • Smile, make eye contact, and introduce yourself
  • Sit up straight and speak clearly
  • Give examples, not just “I’m a hard worker”
  • Ask 1 or 2 questions at the end
  • Say thank you, and send a thank-you email within 24 hours

✉️ Thank-you email template

Subject: Thank you for the interview

Dear (Ms./Mr. + last name),

Thank you for taking the time to meet with me today about the position. I enjoyed learning about .

I am excited about the chance to join your team. I think my and would help me do a great job. Please let me know if you need anything else from me.

Sincerely,
(your full name)
(your phone number)

Use a professional email address, check spelling, and do not use slang or emojis.

📍 Where teens can find jobs near Tulare and Visalia

  • Your school’s career center, counselor, or work-permit office. Ask about local job openings, internships, and how to get your work permit.
  • Employment Connection, Tulare County’s job centers, run by the Workforce Investment Board of Tulare County. Ask about free help with resumes and job searches, and about programs for young people.
  • CalJOBS (caljobs.ca.gov), California’s free online job board from the state.
  • City job pages. Search online for the City of Tulare or City of Visalia jobs page. Cities often hire teens for summer recreation jobs like camp helpers and lifeguards.
  • Local businesses. Look for “Now Hiring” signs, ask for the manager, and bring a resume. Tell family, neighbors, coaches, and your church that you are looking.
  • Big job websites and store websites. Many grocery stores, restaurants, and retail stores take applications online.
🚩 Stay safe: a real job never asks you to pay to apply. Do not share your Social Security number or bank information until you are hired and filling out official paperwork. Under 18? You will usually need a California work permit (forms B1-1 and B1-4) from your school before you start.
